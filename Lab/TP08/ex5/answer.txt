Exercise 5 - Answers

1. Repeated values:
   - Employee info (EID, Name, Gender, DeptID, DeptName) repeats once per skill.
     E01 Dara appears in two rows (Java and Python).
     E03 Vanna appears in two rows (SQL and Python).
   - Department info (DeptID, DeptName) repeats once per employee in that department.
     D1, Sales appears in three rows (E01 Java, E01 Python, E02 Java).

2. Problem with repeated department information:
   If the department name changes (e.g., "Sales" is renamed to "Sales & Marketing"),
   every row containing D1 must be updated (3 rows in this example). If any row is
   missed, the table becomes inconsistent — D1 maps to two different names.
   This is an UPDATE ANOMALY caused by redundant department data.

3. Better decomposition:

   Employee(EID, Name, Gender, DeptID)
   Department(DeptID, DeptName)
   EmployeeSkill(EID, Skill)

   --- Employee table ---
   EID | Name   | Gender | DeptID
   ----|--------|--------|-------
   E01 | Dara   | M      | D1
   E02 | Sophea | F      | D1
   E03 | Vanna  | M      | D2

   --- Department table ---
   DeptID | DeptName
   -------|----------
   D1     | Sales
   D2     | IT

   --- EmployeeSkill table ---
   EID | Skill
   ----|-------
   E01 | Java
   E01 | Python
   E02 | Java
   E03 | SQL
   E03 | Python

   Now renaming a department requires updating exactly 1 row in Department.
   Employee personal info is stored once per person, not once per skill.
